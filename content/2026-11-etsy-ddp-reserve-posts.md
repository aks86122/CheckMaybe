## Topic 2: "Etsy is holding half my money": payment account reserves
From the thread: a 5-year shop with a 99% on-time rate got 50% of sales held for 3 months after a "shipping delay" flag; appeal lifted it after a month.

What secondary sources say (Alura academy; Retail Gazette, VAR, 2021 to 2023 coverage):
- A reserve holds a set percentage of new sales for a set period; both show in Shop Manager > Finances > Payment account, "Payment account reserve" section, only when a reserve is active. Orders show an "Order in reserve" badge. [VERIFY]
- Triggers listed by Etsy (per coverage): sudden sales increase, a shop's first sales, a policy violation, "other risk factors"; Alura adds shipments without tracking, shipping delays, more refund requests. [VERIFY]
- Release: adding tracking that Etsy can verify releases held funds for that order; otherwise at the end of the holding period. [VERIFY]
- 2023: Etsy said it would substantially reduce reserve percentages for most sellers after backlash. Older reports: up to 75% for up to 90 days (2021 to 2023; may be outdated).

### Substack post (short)
Title: `Selling to the US from outside it? What Etsy's DDP rule actually says (and what it doesn't)`
Subtitle: `A rumor says Etsy hides non-US shops that don't prepay duties. Here's the rule, in Etsy's words.`
```
A comment I keep seeing on r/EtsyCommunity: if your shop is outside the US and you don't ship Delivered Duty Paid, Etsy quietly cuts your US traffic.

I went to the two places Etsy published this rule: the June 9 forum announcement by James Ossman, its VP of Customer Operations, and the Seller Handbook article "Navigating Evolving Global Tariff Policies" from the same day.

The rule is real. From the announcement:

> "Starting July 9, you'll be expected to account for all of your costs, including duties and import fees, to US buyers upfront in your pricing by using a Delivered Duty Paid (DDP) shipping option, so they pay the full cost of their order at checkout."

The penalty is spelled out too, and it's about money, not search. The Handbook says "Etsy will enforce this policy change through Etsy Purchase Protection coverage." If you ship with duties unpaid and "the order arrives late or doesn't arrive, you may be responsible for the refund if the buyer opens a case." The announcement adds that if a carrier charges the buyer tariffs or collection fees, the buyer is "eligible to be refunded and the charges will be deducted from the seller."

What I couldn't find is the traffic part. Neither page says listings are hidden or ranked lower for non-DDP sellers. That doesn't prove search isn't affected, but it isn't what Etsy has written down, so I wouldn't plan around it.

There is one exception. "Exceptions apply only in countries where DDP services are not available." If that's you, you have to say in the listing that duties aren't included and will be due on collection, and "message your buyer with the estimated total duty and carrier fee amount and get their agreement before you ship." Keep that message. With proof of it, Etsy says "you may not be responsible for the refund."

Two more lines worth knowing. Digital listings are out of it: "Tariffs apply to physical items, so digital listings are not subject to additional tariffs." And you can opt out of the US entirely: "Sellers may choose which countries they ship to, including choosing to ship only domestically."

If you sell physical items to the US from elsewhere, here's the short version. Check Etsy's list of DDP carriers for your country. Use the US tariff estimator in Shop Manager as a starting point only, since Etsy itself says it "does not guarantee the accuracy or reliability of these estimates." Set US-specific prices so your other markets don't pay for US duties. And if DDP isn't available where you are, follow the disclosure and message steps above, every time.

Rules change, so check Etsy's current pages yourself. This is educational, not legal advice, and I'm not affiliated with Etsy.
```
