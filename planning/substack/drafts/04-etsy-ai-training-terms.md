## Title
Etsy's new Terms let it train AI on your listings. Here's what the opt-out actually covers.

## Subtitle
Three Etsy documents, all updated on 26 October. I read them so you can decide before 16 December.

## Body
```
Etsy published a new Terms of Use on 26 October. It takes effect on 16 December 2026, and one sentence in it has sellers asking how to opt out.

Here's the sentence, from section 5.C, the part where you give Etsy a license to Your Content:

> "This also includes the right for us and our third party partners and vendors to use Your Content to develop, train, test and improve artificial intelligence and machine learning technologies, including to improve the Services and/or help keep them safe, subject to any applicable opt-out rights described in our Privacy Policy."

Two words earlier in the same section matter just as much. The license is "irrevocable" and "perpetual".

What counts as Your Content? Section 5 lists it: "shop names, profile pictures, listing photos, listing descriptions, reviews, comments, videos, usernames, etc."

The sentence points to "opt-out rights described in our Privacy Policy", so I went there next. The new Privacy Policy puts AI training under Etsy's legitimate interests, with this example:

> "We may also use Content you upload to our Services, as described in Section 5 of our Terms of Use, such as listing information, product reviews, and community posts to train and develop artificial intelligence and machine learning models."

And this is the opt-out it means:

> "In the EEA, UK, and other countries you have the right to object to, and seek the restriction of, this processing."

That's a right to object, not a switch. Section 7 of the same policy says that after an objection, Etsy "will stop processing your information unless we have compelling legitimate grounds to continue processing or where it is needed for legal reasons." I didn't find an AI setting anywhere in the policy, and it doesn't say which "other countries" are included.

US sellers have one more document, the US Regional Privacy Policy, also updated on 26 October. Its "Opt-out rights" list two things: processing for targeted advertising, and "sales" of personal data. AI training isn't on that list, and the page doesn't mention AI at all.

What about closing your shop? Section 7 of the Terms says that "Terminating your account will not affect the availability of some of Your Content that you posted through the Services prior to termination." Section 13 says that if you don't agree with changes to the Terms, "you may close your account." It doesn't say closing undoes a license that's "irrevocable" and "perpetual".

One thing to keep apart: the Terms also mention a 30 day opt-out in section 11.G. That one is about arbitration, it's only for new users, and it has nothing to do with AI training.

The same update also adds section 6.E, which says nobody else may use content from Etsy to train AI "without our express permission." So the rule runs one way.

If you sell on Etsy, here's what I'd do before 16 December. Read section 5 of the Terms and the "legitimate interests" part of the Privacy Policy yourself. If you're in the EEA or UK and want to object, the Privacy Policy lists dpo@etsy.com among its contacts. And decide what you upload with the license in mind, because the license follows what you post.

Rules change, so check the current pages yourself. This is educational, not legal advice, and I'm not affiliated with Etsy.

Separately, if you sell anything made with AI on Etsy, the October 2026 listing rules changed too (disclosure, previews, prompt bundles, AI-written patterns and recipes). I summed those up on one page in Can I Use This?:
https://checkmaybe.gumroad.com/l/kzhzh?utm_source=substack&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=etsy-ai-terms
```
