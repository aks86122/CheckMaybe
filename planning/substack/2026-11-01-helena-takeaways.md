# Takeaways: Helena Di Biase, "How to start on Substack and get…" (read 2026-11-01)

Source: https://helenadibiase.substack.com/p/how-to-start-on-substack-and-get (J shared). She sells a Substack setup book and a paid tier, so her numbers are marketing: 520 subscribers in 30 days from daily Notes, 22,000 free in 9 months, "99% organic". Unverified; she started with existing Instagram/TikTok audiences.

## What we adopt
1. **Notes every day**, not 3 to 5 a week. She credits Notes for most growth ("Notes are how you get discovered by people who don't already follow you"). Our backlog already covers ~10 days (GOLD RUSH, DDP hype check, reserve, human-made, Etsy terms follow-ups). Plus 2 to 3 genuine replies a day on other sellers' Notes.
2. **Value proposition line** ("I help [audience] achieve [outcome] by providing [value]") → new short description below.
3. **Sections** so the publication reads like a magazine: "Can I sell it?" (rule explainers), "Hype check" (claim vs the rule text), "Build log" (our numbers).
4. **Welcome email points to the best entry post**, not only the freebie. Best entry post today: the Etsy AI-training terms post.
5. **Every post gets promoted**: one pin + one Note excerpt per post, link to the post itself (already our plan).
6. **Keyword headlines**: Substack posts rank in Google; keep titles in the words sellers search ("Etsy payment reserve", "Etsy DDP").

## What we don't copy
- 2 to 3 posts a week with a paid tier: too early (1 subscriber). Stay at 2 posts a week, free.
- "Ask your network for the first 50 to 100": J's network is Taiwanese and not Etsy sellers; skip.
- Cross-promotion / guest posts: her month 4 to 6 step; revisit when we have ~200 subscribers.
