## Title
What "personal use only" actually blocks

## Subtitle
It isn't one rule. It means whatever the license in front of you says.

## Body
```
"For personal use only."

You'll see it on freebie packs, on Etsy downloads, and on some of the content inside Canva. Most sellers read it as "you can't sell this", and that's close. But the phrase has no official meaning of its own. It's a trade term, so what it blocks depends on the license that uses it.

Canva is a useful example because it spells its limits out. In its Content License Agreement, section 5B says Branded Content is for "personal use only", and section 5C says Education Content is for "non-commercial use only". Section 1 adds the part that catches sellers: when a design mixes content with different licenses, the most restrictive one "applies to the entire design".

So one branded or education element in an otherwise clean template can make the whole design something you can't sell. It doesn't matter how small the element is or how much of your own work sits around it.

Before you list anything built from other people's content, open the info panel of every element you didn't make yourself and look for words like branded, education, editorial or personal use. If you bought a pack labeled "personal use", read the license file that came with it, not only the label. Until that license says otherwise, the safe reading is that you can't sell it, use it for a client, or use it to promote your shop.

Rules change, so check the current pages yourself. This is educational, not legal advice, and I'm not affiliated with Canva.
```
