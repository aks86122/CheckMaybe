## Topic 1: "Does Etsy hide non-US shops from US buyers?" (Hype check)
Claim in the thread (one commenter): a non-US shop without DDP shipping gets its US traffic restricted, and US buyers may see a note that duties might be due.

What secondary sources say (Value Added Resource, Shopifreaks, Nova Data; 2026-06):
- VERIFIED 2026-11-01 (forum announcement "Upcoming policy changes for orders shipped to the US", James Ossman, posted Jun 9, 2026, pasted by J):
  - "Starting July 9, you'll be expected to account for all of your costs, including duties and import fees, to US buyers upfront in your pricing by using a Delivered Duty Paid (DDP) shipping option, so they pay the full cost of their order at checkout."
  - "Orders shipped without duties prepaid will no longer qualify for Etsy Purchase Protection. If buyers are charged tariffs or collection fees by the carrier, they're eligible to be refunded and the charges will be deducted from the seller."
  - Reason given: "nearly two-thirds of US Etsy buyers said they'd be unlikely to make a purchase if they had to pay tariffs at the time of delivery." (footnote: survey of 833 US buyers, July 2025)
  - Policies updated: Seller Policy, Off-Platform Transactions, Cases, Shipping, Purchase Protection Program for Sellers.
  - The announcement says nothing about search ranking or hiding listings from US buyers.
- Exception reported elsewhere: countries where DDP services aren't available; then the listing must disclose that duties are due on delivery. [VERIFY]
- Tools: US tariffs estimator in Shop Manager; separate domestic / US / international pricing. [VERIFY]
- **Search visibility: no source found.** None of the coverage says non-DDP listings are hidden or down-ranked. The post must say that plainly unless Etsy's own pages say otherwise.

## Topic 2: "Etsy is holding half my money": payment account reserves
From the thread: a 5-year shop with a 99% on-time rate got 50% of sales held for 3 months after a "shipping delay" flag; appeal lifted it after a month.

What secondary sources say (Alura academy; Retail Gazette, VAR, 2021 to 2023 coverage):
- A reserve holds a set percentage of new sales for a set period; both show in Shop Manager > Finances > Payment account, "Payment account reserve" section, only when a reserve is active. Orders show an "Order in reserve" badge. [VERIFY]
- Triggers listed by Etsy (per coverage): sudden sales increase, a shop's first sales, a policy violation, "other risk factors"; Alura adds shipments without tracking, shipping delays, more refund requests. [VERIFY]
- Release: adding tracking that Etsy can verify releases held funds for that order; otherwise at the end of the holding period. [VERIFY]
- 2023: Etsy said it would substantially reduce reserve percentages for most sellers after backlash. Older reports: up to 75% for up to 90 days (2021 to 2023; may be outdated).
- **Appeal / contact route: not found in coverage.** Must come from Etsy's own page or be stated as not found.

Pages J needs to paste:
All three pasted by J 2026-11-01 (Help Center "What is a Payment Account Reserve?"; Etsy Payments Policy "Last updated on Jul 31, 2026"; Seller Handbook "How We Use Payment Account Reserve to Keep Etsy Safe", August 4, 2023).

### Verified quotes
Help Center:
- "a percentage of funds from each new sale of a physical item will be held and won't be available for deposit within the standard processing time."
- Released when "The seller adds tracking to the order, and Etsy can verify the tracking shows the order is in transit" or "a default holding period of time has passed."
- "Sales of digital items are not held in reserve."
- "We regularly evaluate accounts with a Payment account reserve to determine whether the reserve is still needed." ... "Shipping orders within your processing times, providing good customer service, and keeping your shop in good standing can help demonstrate that a reserve is no longer needed."
- Risk factors listed: sudden sharp increase in orders or refunds; orders consistently missing tracking; "Orders aren't shipped on time"; recent increase in refunds; first sale.
- "If you're already a Star Seller, a reserve will not be placed on your shop."
- "Etsy isn't always able to confirm tracking for some carriers."
- Funds available "at 6pm ET on the day they're released"; see dates in Recent activities, monthly statement CSV ("Status", "Availability Date"), "Order in reserve" badge in Orders.
Payments Policy §7B:
- Reserve email "will include details of the Reserve, including the Reserve percentage applied to your Payment Account, the clearing window for individual payments, and the basis and timing on which your Reserve will be reviewed."
- Example (illustrative): "your Reserve could be set at 30% and held for a 45-day rolling period"
- Variable reserve: delayed "for a period of up to 180 days" or deducted "as a reserve for a period of up to 90 days"
- Holds: "for the shorter of: (a) 180 days after the estimated delivery date, or (b) completion of any investigation"; deposit delay "between 0 and 180 days"
- Reduction/removal possible "as a result of a seller's positive performance, such as a reduction in cases or an improvement in the seller's fulfillment of orders."
- "Etsy is not obligated to refund any fees or reimburse any expenses due to holds or delayed deposits."
Seller Handbook (2023):
- "Even if a reserve is placed on your account, your shop and listings are still active and visible to shoppers and you can continue to make sales."
- Email comes "from support@etsy.com".
- Two most common reasons: missing tracking "especially for orders over $100" and "Orders aren't shipped on time"; also "made a sale after a long period of inactivity", "history of low reviews".
- No formal appeal form described in any of the three; the route is re-evaluation + contacting support (Help Center "Contact support").

### Substack post

```
A seller with five years on Etsy and a 99% on-time rate posted on r/EtsyCommunity that Etsy held half of every sale for three months over shipping delays. If you've just had the email, here's what Etsy's own pages say, in their words.

What a reserve is. From Etsy's Help Center: "a percentage of funds from each new sale of a physical item will be held and won't be available for deposit within the standard processing time." Not your whole balance, and not digital sales: "Sales of digital items are not held in reserve."

How big and how long. It varies, and the email tells you. The Etsy Payments Policy says that email "will include details of the Reserve, including the Reserve percentage applied to your Payment Account, the clearing window for individual payments, and the basis and timing on which your Reserve will be reviewed." The policy's own example is 30% for a 45-day rolling period, and it allows a variable reserve "for a period of up to 90 days." So keep that email. It's the closest thing you'll get to terms.

Why it happened. Etsy's 2023 Seller Handbook article names the two most common reasons: orders "consistently missing tracking information or tracking events, especially for orders over $100" and "Orders aren't shipped on time." Other triggers it lists include a sudden jump in orders or refunds, a first sale, "a sale after a long period of inactivity", and a history of low reviews.

Your shop isn't hidden. Same article: "Even if a reserve is placed on your account, your shop and listings are still active and visible to shoppers and you can continue to make sales." If your traffic dropped at the same time, the reserve isn't what Etsy says caused it.

The fastest way out, per order. Add tracking. Funds are released when "Etsy can verify the tracking shows the order is in transit." The catch: "Etsy isn't always able to confirm tracking for some carriers," and then that order waits out the full period. You can see each order's release date in your monthly statement CSV, under "Availability Date".

Getting the reserve itself lifted. I didn't find an appeal form for reserves in any of the three pages. What Etsy says is that it "regularly evaluate[s] accounts with a Payment account reserve to determine whether the reserve is still needed", and that a reserve can be reduced or removed after "a reduction in cases or an improvement in the seller's fulfillment of orders." So the practical route is to fix whatever the email names, then contact Etsy Support and ask for a review, with your tracking and on-time numbers ready. And one line worth knowing before you plan around it: "If you're already a Star Seller, a reserve will not be placed on your shop."

Rules change, so check Etsy's current pages yourself. This is educational, not financial or legal advice, and I'm not affiliated with Etsy.
```

### Substack post (short)
Title: `Selling to the US from outside it? What Etsy's DDP rule actually says (and what it doesn't)`
Subtitle: `A rumor says Etsy hides non-US shops that don't prepay duties. Here's the rule, in Etsy's words.`
```
A comment on r/EtsyCommunity last week: if your shop is outside the US and you don't ship Delivered Duty Paid, Etsy quietly cuts your US traffic.

I went to the two places Etsy published this rule: the June 9 forum announcement by James Ossman, its VP of Customer Operations, and the Seller Handbook article "Navigating Evolving Global Tariff Policies" from the same day.

The rule is real. From the announcement:

> "Starting July 9, you'll be expected to account for all of your costs, including duties and import fees, to US buyers upfront in your pricing by using a Delivered Duty Paid (DDP) shipping option, so they pay the full cost of their order at checkout."

The penalty is spelled out too, and it's about money, not search. The Handbook says "Etsy will enforce this policy change through Etsy Purchase Protection coverage." If you ship with duties unpaid and "the order arrives late or doesn't arrive, you may be responsible for the refund if the buyer opens a case." The announcement adds that if a carrier charges the buyer tariffs or collection fees, the buyer is "eligible to be refunded and the charges will be deducted from the seller."

What I couldn't find is the traffic part. Neither page says listings are hidden or ranked lower for non-DDP sellers. That doesn't prove search isn't affected, but it isn't what Etsy has written down, so I wouldn't plan around it.

There is one exception. "Exceptions apply only in countries where DDP services are not available." If that's you, you have to say in the listing that duties aren't included and will be due on collection, and "message your buyer with the estimated total duty and carrier fee amount and get their agreement before you ship." Keep that message. With proof of it, Etsy says "you may not be responsible for the refund."

Two more lines worth knowing. Digital listings are out of it: "Tariffs apply to physical items, so digital listings are not subject to additional tariffs." And you can opt out of the US entirely: "Sellers may choose which countries they ship to, including choosing to ship only domestically."

Before you lean on that exception, check Etsy's carrier table in the Help Center article "Information for Managing International Shipments". Many countries aren't listed by name, and "Rest of the World" still gets DHL, FedEx and UPS as DDP carriers. So for most sellers DDP is available, just not always cheap. I'm in Taiwan, which isn't listed by name either, so I'd fall under "Rest of the World" too.

If you sell physical items to the US from elsewhere, here's the short version. Find your country, or "Rest of the World", in that table. Use the US tariff calculator in Shop Manager as a starting point only: its estimates "do not include additional fees your carrier may charge, such as customs clearance, brokerage, or administrative costs," it's "currently unavailable for listings with price variations," and its HTS codes come from AI, with Etsy saying it "does not guarantee the accuracy or reliability" of them. Set US-specific prices so your other markets don't pay for US duties. And if DDP truly isn't available where you are, follow the disclosure and message steps above, every time.

Rules change, so check Etsy's current pages yourself. This is educational, not legal advice, and I'm not affiliated with Etsy.
```
