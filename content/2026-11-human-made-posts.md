## Substack post

### Title
Made it by hand and still flagged as AI? What Etsy asks for, and what to keep

### Subtitle
Etsy's October photo rules in plain words, and a five-minute habit that gives you proof.

### Body
```
A seller asked on Reddit last week: if someone reports my listing as AI and it isn't, how do I defend myself?

Another said a background she removed in Canva got her post labelled as AI on Instagram. A third, who test stitches every pattern by hand, can't get buyers to notice that her work is human-made at all.

Here's what Etsy actually asks for, why handmade work can still read as AI, and what I'd keep from now on.

What Etsy asks for. [VERIFY] Etsy's Listing Image Requirements, updated on 22 October 2026, ask most listings to include at least one unedited photo of the actual item on a real background. Your other photos can still be edited, even with AI tools, as long as they show the item accurately. Digital downloads should show a preview or excerpt of what the buyer gets. And in the same update Etsy said that new reports about AI images won't lead to immediate enforcement at first.

That unedited photo is your best evidence. It's the one picture on the listing that says: this is the real thing, as it is.

Why your own photo can read as AI. Some editing tools write a small record into the image file, called Content Credentials or C2PA, and Instagram and Facebook say they read these records for their AI label. When I tested files, an image from ChatGPT and one edited with Canva's AI tools both carried an AI marker. Plain edits in Canva and images saved from a chat app carried no data at all, and photos straight from a phone kept their camera details. So the label can come from the tool you used on the file, not from how you made the item.

What I'd keep. For every finished piece, keep the original phone photo untouched, in a folder by date, and use a copy of it as your unedited listing photo. Take one or two quick photos while you work, the half-finished piece on your table. Keep your source files, the chart, the sketch, the first draft, with their dates. And write down which app you used on your other listing photos. It takes five minutes per item, and if a listing is ever questioned, you have the answer ready instead of hoping someone believes you.

What I couldn't find. I haven't found an Etsy page that explains how to respond if your listing is reported as AI. If you've been through it, I'd really like to know how it went. Reply or leave a comment.

If you want to see what your photo file says before you upload it, the free CheckMaybe Photo Check reads it in your browser. Your photo is never uploaded.
https://checkmaybe-photo-check.pages.dev/?utm_source=substack&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=human-made

Rules change, so check Etsy's current pages yourself. This is educational, not legal advice, and I'm not affiliated with Etsy, Canva or Meta.
```
