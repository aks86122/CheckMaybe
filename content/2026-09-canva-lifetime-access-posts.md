## Facebook (full length)
```
You can find “lifetime Canva Pro” on marketplaces for a few dollars, often as a “team invite”.

If you only make birthday cards, that’s between you and Canva. If you plan to sell templates or printables made with it, read these lines from Canva’s own terms first.

Canva Terms of Use (effective 19 August 2026), §2e. You will not “rent, lease, sell, distribute, offer in a service bureau, sublicense, or otherwise make available the Service or the Licensed Content to any third party (except as permitted under these Terms)”.

§8, on Teams: “the Team Owner or Administrator may control access to, delete, or re-assign ownership to the User Content you upload and Designs you create on the account you access as part of the Team.”

Content License Agreement §4: Pro Content Licenses are issued “at no additional cost if you have a valid Canva subscription.” And §10: if an account is terminated for “any abuse of a username or password”, you “lose all rights to Content and Canva designs”.

So before you sell anything made with Canva, check three things: whose account it is, who pays for the subscription, and whether someone else can remove you or your designs. If it’s a Canva Education account, Pro Content is for educational, non-commercial use only (CLA §8).

Educational information, not legal advice. Rules change, so check Canva’s current terms yourself. Not affiliated with Canva.
```
